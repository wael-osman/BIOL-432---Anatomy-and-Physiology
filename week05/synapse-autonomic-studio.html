Layla — the sweat-gland puzzle

During her heat collapse, Layla's body ran a full sympathetic surge: racing heart, blood shunted to muscle — and sweating. Yet the rule you just learned says sympathetic postganglionic fibres release norepinephrine, while sweating is driven by acetylcholine.

The closing MPT prompt: which receptor type let Layla keep sweating, even though sweating is a sympathetic response?

Type or think your answer, then reveal the model response.

Muscarinic cholinergic receptors. Sweat glands are the famous exception: although they are sympathetic, their postganglionic fibres are cholinergic — they release acetylcholine, which acts on muscarinic receptors on the gland. So Layla sweated through a sympathetic pathway that happens to use the parasympathetic transmitter.

Follow-up to pose the room: if you blocked muscarinic receptors with atropine, what would happen to her sweating? (It would stop — atropine dries secretions.)
