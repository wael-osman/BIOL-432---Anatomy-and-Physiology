Layla — why did the room spin?

When Layla stood up after collapsing, she felt dizzy and unsteady, as if the room were spinning. Her hearing was fine. Something in her sensory system was reporting motion that her eyes couldn't confirm.

The closing MPT prompt: which receptor organ explains Layla's spinning sensation — and what was it actually detecting?

Think it through, then reveal the model answer.

The vestibular apparatus — not the cochlea. Her spinning sensation came from the inner-ear balance organs: the semicircular canals (cristae ampullares) detecting rotational acceleration, and the utricle and saccule (maculae) detecting linear acceleration and head position relative to gravity. They were reporting motion and tilt — not sound.

The clincher to add: combined with a blood-pressure drop as she stood, her balance organs and baroreceptors were briefly overwhelmed at once — so the room seemed to spin. Hearing was spared because the cochlea is a separate chamber of the same labyrinth.
